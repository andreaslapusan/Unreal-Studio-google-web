This page explains where the "projected ROI 15-28% per year" range used by Unreal Studio Bali comes from, which assumptions sit behind it, how gross becomes net, and what can make the real result worse. Gross yield, net yield and ROI are three different things

Many Bali websites mix these terms, which is why their numbers seem to contradict each other.

TermWhat it measuresFormula
Gross yieldRental income against priceAnnual gross income ÷ purchase price
Net (operating) yieldWhat is left after rental costs(Gross income − running costs) ÷ purchase price
Net yield after taxWhat reaches youNet operating income − income tax
Total ROIThe whole life of the investmentCumulative net rent ± change in value of the right ÷ total investment

The 15-28% range is projected gross yield. Whenever someone quotes a percentage, ask whether it is gross or net, and on what base.

The formula we use

``` Annual gross income = average daily rate (ADR) × occupancy × 365 Gross yield = annual gross income ÷ purchase price ```

  • ADR (average daily rate): the average price a guest pays per night, before taxes.
  • Occupancy: the share of nights in the year the unit is booked.
  • Purchase price: the unit price in the contract. If you add furniture, notary and other buying costs, the base is larger and the percentage falls.

Holiday-rental returns depend on two variables nobody fully controls: how much you can charge per night and how many nights you fill. That is why we work with scenarios, not a single number.

Step 4: the cost of time in a leasehold

A leasehold is a right of use for a set number of years. At the end of the term, unless you extend it, the right returns to the landowner. To compare it with a freehold purchase, remember that part of each year's rent is really paying back your capital:

Length of the rightShare of rent that recovers the capital (€100,000)
25 years€4,000/year (4%)

70 years€1,430/year (1.4%)

That is why the term matters. Our projects are sold as leaseholds signed before a notary, with terms that vary by plot: for example, Amante Villas offers a 70-year leasehold or freehold at the same price. Taxes: what to deduct

Tax depends on how the unit is rented (personally, through a manager or through an Indonesian PT PMA company) and on where you are tax resident. In general:

  • In Indonesia: tourist accommodation pays a regional tax (PBJT), usually 10% in Bali and normally passed on in the guest's rate. Rental income is subject to Indonesian income tax under the structure you choose.
  • In your home country: most countries tax residents on worldwide income, and many have double-tax treaties with Indonesia that let you credit tax paid there.

Always ask a tax adviser for a calculation before you buy. This page is not tax advice.

What can go wrong

A projection is only useful if you know what breaks it. These are the risks that affect real returns most:

  1. Lower occupancy than assumed. Bali has a high season (July to September and year-end) and a low season (February to March). In 2025, star-rated hotel occupancy was 46.61% in March and 69.54% in August (BPS Bali).
  2. Falling rates as supply grows. Many new villas are entering the market. AirDNA shows a year-on-year fall in Bali's average daily rate to July 2026.
  3. Ramp-up year. A new unit needs months and reviews to fill up. The first year usually falls short of the base scenario.
  4. Construction delays. A unit earns nothing until it is handed over. Track the build and read the contract: see buying off-plan in Bali.
  5. Regulation. Holiday-rental licences, zoning and taxes can change. In September 2025 the Bali government announced a regulation against building villas and hotels on productive farmland.
  6. Currency. Guests pay in rupiah or dollars while you measure the investment in euros or your own currency.
  7. Management. A poor manager can sink occupancy and the condition of the villa. Read how remote villa management works in Bali.
  8. Leasehold term and extension. Check how many years remain and on what terms the lease can be extended.

How to read any Bali yield figure

Before comparing one developer with another, ask five questions:

  • Is it gross or net? Net of which costs?
  • What nightly rate and occupancy does it assume?
  • What price is it calculated on: the unit alone or the total investment?
  • How many years of leasehold remain?
  • Is it based on real comparable units or on a theoretical calculation?

If any answer is unclear, the number is not good enough to decide on.

Frequently asked questions

What rental yield does a villa in Bali make?

It depends on the nightly rate, occupancy and price. In our scenarios for a one-bedroom unit, projected gross yield ranges from 15% to 28% per year and net operating yield, before income tax, from 7.5% to 18%. These are projections, not guarantees.

Is the 15-28% gross or net?

It is projected gross yield: rental income before costs, divided by the purchase price. Net yield, after platform commissions, management, utilities and maintenance, is lower.

What occupancy is realistic in Bali?

For new, well-managed units we assume 55% to 77% depending on type and scenario. For reference, star-rated hotels in Bali averaged 61.02% occupancy in 2025, according to BPS. The first year is usually lower.

How much does a villa manager charge in Bali?

Usually 15% to 25% of rental income, depending on the services included. Booking platform commissions, utilities and maintenance come on top.

Are the returns guaranteed?

No. They are projections based on rate, occupancy and cost assumptions. Actual results may be lower, nil or negative, and depend on tourist demand, management, regulation, tax and exchange rates.

Can I get a mortgage to boost the return?

No. There are no mortgages or loans in Bali for this kind of foreign buyer. What spreads the cost is a staged payment schedule tied to construction milestones, from your own funds.

Why don't you publish the rent for each project?

Because the final rent depends on how each unit is managed, its handover date and the market at that time. We publish the method and the assumptions by property type so you can check the calculation yourself.

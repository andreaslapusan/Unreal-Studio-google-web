Canggu is the area that put Bali on the map for digital nomads: surf beaches, cafés, coworking spaces, gyms and nightlife that runs all year. It sits on the south-west coast, north of Seminyak. Further west the same coastline continues through Pererenan, Seseh and Cemagi, three villages that were rice fields a few years ago and now absorb much of the demand Canggu can no longer meet.

This guide explains the market on each stretch of the coast, the projects we have done here, and who it makes sense for to invest in the area today.

Canggu today: strong demand, a lot of supply

Canggu has the steadiest demand in Bali: people who come for one to three months, work remotely and rent a villa with a pool. That is good for investors, but the full picture matters:

  • There is a lot of supply. Thousands of villas have been built in Canggu in a few years. Competition on nightly rates is high, and a villa with nothing to set it apart falls behind.
  • Traffic is the main problem. Streets are narrow and rush-hour jams are daily. For guests, being within walking distance of everything matters more here than elsewhere.
  • Land is expensive. Land prices in central Canggu (Berawa, Batu Bolong) are already at Seminyak levels. The entry ticket for a small investor is higher than in Uluwatu.

That is why the market is moving west, where the coast still has rice fields and entry prices are lower.

Pererenan

Pererenan lies just west of Echo Beach, 5-10 minutes from central Canggu. In a few years it has filled with cafés, restaurants and gyms, with a calmer, more family feel than Canggu. Its dark volcanic-sand beach is for surfing and sunsets. For guests it offers the best of both: village life on foot with Canggu close by.

Seseh and Cemagi

Further west along the coast are Seseh and Cemagi: rice fields running down to the sea, black-sand beaches with strong currents (better for watching and surfing than swimming) and some of the best sunsets on the island. There are fewer services within walking distance than in Pererenan, but more cafés, gyms and padel clubs open every year. This is the area for larger villas with open views, aimed at families and groups.

Rentals: how demand behaves

  • Long stays all year. Canggu has more one- to three-month guests than anywhere else in Bali. That smooths the low season and reduces turnover (fewer cleans and check-ins).
  • Peaks in July-August and at Christmas. As elsewhere on the island, these are the months with the highest short-stay rates.
  • What rents best: one- and two-bedroom villas with private pools and a workspace for remote workers; three- and four-bedroom villas in Seseh and Cemagi for families and groups.
  • What sets a villa apart: original design, a good internet connection, parking and walking distance to cafés or the beach.

Who it suits

  • It suits you if you want an area with life all year round, accept a higher entry price and are targeting long-stay guests.
  • It suits you less if your budget is below about EUR 150,000 or you want the calm of a less built-up area. In that case, look at Uluwatu or Tabanan.

How payment works

Bank financing is not available in Bali for a foreign buyer in this kind of purchase. New builds are paid in milestones during construction, with each payment tied to a stage and documented with reports and photos.

What to check before buying in Canggu

  1. Land use. Part of Badung's rice fields is protected for agriculture. For nightly rentals the plot must be in a zone that allows tourist use. Ask for the zoning document.
  2. Building permit (PBG). The villa should have one, or the application should be filed.
  3. Access. In Canggu some villas sit at the end of lanes no car can enter. Access matters for guests and for resale value.
  4. What will be built next door. An unprotected rice field can become a building site tomorrow. Open views are only worth paying for if they are protected.
  5. Leasehold years. Count the years from handover and check the extension terms. More in leasehold vs freehold in Indonesia.

Frequently asked questions

Is Canggu still a good place to invest?

Canggu has the steadiest demand in Bali, but also a lot of supply and expensive land. Today Pererenan, Seseh or Cemagi, or a villa with something that sets it apart, make more sense than a standard villa in central Canggu.

What is the difference between Canggu and Pererenan?

Pererenan is 5-10 minutes west of Canggu. It has cafés and restaurants on foot, but it is calmer, more family-oriented and land is somewhat cheaper.

How far is Canggu from the airport?

About 20-25 km. It takes between 45 minutes and an hour and a half, depending on traffic.

Is it legal to rent a villa nightly in Canggu?

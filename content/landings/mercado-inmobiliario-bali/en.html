Tourism: a record 2025 and a flatter 2026

International arrivals

In 2025, 6,948,754 foreign tourists arrived directly in Bali, 9.72% more than in 2024, according to Bali's statistics office (BPS Bali), which released the figure on 2 February 2026. It is the highest number on record.

The main source markets in 2025 were:

Source: BPS Bali, reported by Antara on 3 February 2026. Shares for India and China are our own calculation on the total.

2026 has been flatter. Between January and July, 3,900,965 foreign tourists arrived, 1.98% fewer than in the same months of 2025. July brought 697,809, almost the same as a year earlier (+0.10%), and was the first month of 2026 with year-on-year growth. BPS Bali expects August and September to be the strongest months of the year. Source: BPS Bali, reported by Antara on 1 September 2026.

What it means for investors: demand is at record levels, but 2026 is not growing like 2025. An investment plan should not assume arrivals rise every year.

Airport

I Gusti Ngurah Rai airport handled 18,231,771 passengers between January and September 2025, 1% more than in the same period of 2024. 63% were on international flights. Source: airport figures reported by Kompas on 12 October 2025.

Occupancy: Indonesia's highest, with strong seasonality

Star-rated hotels in Bali averaged 61.02% occupancy in 2025, the highest of any Indonesian province. The national average was 49.30%. Source: BPS, reported by Databoks on 3 February 2026.

Occupancy swings widely through the year. According to BPS Bali's 2025 monthly reports:

Month (2025)Star-rated hotel occupancy
March (low point)46.61%
June64.66%
August (peak)69.54%

In July 2026, star-rated hotel occupancy was 67.29%, and 74.45% for five-star hotels (BPS Bali, 1 Sep 2026).

These are hotel figures, not private villas. They show the trend and the seasonality, but a villa competes in a different segment.

Holiday rentals: more supply, falling rates

According to AirDNA, active short-term rentals in Bali earned on average about USD 12,700 a year at an average rate of about USD 144 per night in the 12 months to July 2026. Compared with the previous year, revenue fell 12.2% and the average rate 11.9%.

Other platforms report very different numbers because they measure different samples. AirROI, for example, shows an average rate of USD 272 and 40.9% occupancy for Bali between July 2025 and June 2026. When comparing rental data, always compare the same source, the same period and the same type of property.

Reading: falling rates at a time of record tourism suggest that accommodation supply has grown faster than demand. Location, design and management matter more than they did two years ago.

Regulation: what has changed

  • Tourist levy (since 14 February 2024): every foreign tourist pays IDR 150,000 per visit to Bali, through the official Love Bali portal.
  • Hotel and villa moratorium (2024-2025): in September 2024 a freeze on new hotels, villas and nightlife venues in south Bali was proposed. In January 2025 the governor dropped a blanket moratorium in favour of stricter control (Skift, January 2025).
  • Farmland (September 2025): after the September 2025 floods, Governor Wayan Koster announced a regional regulation to ban hotels, villas and restaurants on productive agricultural land (The Jakarta Post, 16 Sep 2025). We have not been able to confirm from official sources whether it is already in force.

What it means for investors: always check the land's zoning and the building permit (PBG) before buying. Land zoned for agriculture may not allow tourist use.

What we expect for 2026-2027

Without making numerical forecasts, these are the trends we see in the data:

  1. High but stable demand. Bali is at record international tourism levels, but 2026 is not repeating 2025's growth.
  2. More competition in holiday rentals. Average rates are falling; well-located, well-designed and well-managed units hold up better.
  3. Tighter control of land use. Regulatory pressure on building on farmland makes plots with correct tourism zoning more valuable.
  4. Australia remains the key market, with India and China growing as secondary markets.

Frequently asked questions

How many foreign tourists visit Bali each year?

In 2025, 6,948,754 foreign tourists arrived directly in Bali, 9.72% more than in 2024 and the highest figure on record, according to BPS Bali (published 2 February 2026).

Is Bali tourism growing in 2026?

Less than in 2025. Between January and July 2026, 3,900,965 foreign tourists arrived, 1.98% fewer than a year earlier. July was the first month with year-on-year growth, according to BPS Bali (1 September 2026).

What is hotel occupancy in Bali?

Star-rated hotels in Bali averaged 61.02% occupancy in 2025, the highest in Indonesia, where the average was 49.30% (BPS). It varies strongly by season: from 46.61% in March to 69.54% in August 2025.

Is there a property bubble in Bali?

There is no official price index to confirm or rule it out. What the data does show is that holiday-rental supply has grown and the average nightly rate fell 11.9% in the 12 months to July 2026, according to AirDNA. That makes location and management more important.

How much does land cost in Bali?

According to listings compiled in May 2026, freehold land in prime Canggu costs IDR 1.5 to 3 billion per are, and in Uluwatu IDR 800 million to 1.3 billion. Tabanan is 30-50% below Canggu. Leasehold is much cheaper.

Can villas still be built in Bali?

Yes, on land with the right zoning and a building permit. In September 2025 the Bali government announced a regulation to ban hotels and villas on productive farmland, and the blanket moratorium proposed in 2024 was dropped in January 2025.

Short answer: buying off-plan in Bali means buying a villa or apartment before or during construction. You pay a reservation, sign the contract before a notary and pay the balance in instalments on dates fixed in the contract that follow construction progress; the final payment is due at handover. There is no mortgage or financing: it is a staged payment from your own funds.

It is how most of our clients buy, and the process we know best, because at Unreal Studio Bali we design and build our own projects in Uluwatu, Canggu-Pererenan and Tabanan. This guide explains how it works, what protects you, what the risks are and how to follow construction from abroad.

What does buying off-plan in Bali mean?

Buying off-plan means signing for a unit that does not exist yet or is still being built. What you buy is a right, usually a leasehold signed before a notary, over a specific unit described in the contract: its plot, plans, floor area, finishes and handover date.

In Bali this is the normal way to buy from a developer. Most new projects are sold while under construction, and many sell out before completion.

Advantages of buying off-plan

  • Lower entry price. Developers set their most competitive price at the start, when the buyer waits longest. Prices rise as construction advances.
  • Staged payments. You do not pay the full amount at signing. You spread it across the build, from your own funds and with no interest.
  • Choice of unit. Early buyers get the best positions in the complex.
  • Customisation. At an early stage some finishes or layouts can sometimes be adjusted, depending on the project.
  • A new product. You receive a brand-new unit with a construction warranty, ready to compete in the holiday-rental market.

Risks of buying off-plan (and how to reduce them)

Off-plan has real risks. Knowing them is the best way to avoid them.

Construction delays

The most common risk. In Bali the rainy season, labour availability and permit processing all play a part. To reduce it:

  • The contract should set a specific handover date, a grace period and compensation if it is exceeded.
  • Ask for regular construction reports with dated photos.
  • Check the developer's track record: have they delivered other projects? Can you visit them?

The developer does not finish

The most serious risk. Bali has had stalled projects, especially in downturns. To reduce it:

  • Buy from a developer with a registered Indonesian company and finished projects you can see.
  • Only pay into the account named in the contract and keep every receipt.
  • Prefer payment schedules spread across the build over schedules that front-load almost everything.

Land or permit problems

The land must have a valid certificate (SHM or SHGB), a use compatible with tourist rentals and a permit file under way. To reduce it:

  • The notary and PPAT checks the land certificate and the owner's identity before signing. See what the PPAT notary does in a Bali purchase.
  • Ask about zoning and the status of land-use validation (KKPR), the building permit (PBG) and the certificate of fitness for use (SLF). Indonesian authorities can take months to issue them: what matters is that the use is permitted and the process has started.

The result is not what was promised

  • Insist that the contract includes plans, floor areas and a specification of finishes.
  • Ask exactly what is handed over: what the price includes and what is optional (furniture, equipment).

How payment works: reservation and staged payments

There is no mortgage financing in Bali for foreign leasehold buyers: Indonesian banks do not lend against this right and foreign banks do not accept it as collateral. Off-plan purchases are paid from your own funds, but in stages: each payment has a date fixed in the contract and goes with a phase of construction.

  • The price is fixed in the contract, in the currency stated in it (euros in most of our contracts).
  • Each payment is a bank transfer to the account named in the contract, with a receipt.
  • Each project has its own schedule: check it on the project page and in the contract before reserving.

If you searched for "financing a property in Bali", here is the honest answer: there is no mortgage, but staged payments during construction let you spread the outlay over several months.

What should be in your contract

Before signing, make sure your contract includes at least:

  1. Full identification of the parties: the developer company and the landowner.
  2. Land description: certificate number, your plot area and location.
  3. Unit description: plans, floor areas, finishes and included installations.
  4. Nature and term of the right: how many years of leasehold, from when, and the extension option.
  5. Payment schedule with amounts and dates.
  6. Handover date, grace period and compensation for delays.
  7. Construction warranty: what it covers and for how long.
  8. What happens if the deal does not go ahead for legal reasons or because of the developer: refund of payments made.
  9. The right to sell, assign and inherit the unit.
  10. Governing law and jurisdiction.

Off-plan or finished: how to decide

There is no single answer. It depends on your horizon, your risk tolerance and what you want the villa for.

Off-plan suits you if:

  • Your horizon is several years and you do not need immediate income.
  • You prefer to spread the outlay over the build.
  • You want the lowest entry price and your pick of units.
  • You are comfortable checking the developer and following construction remotely.

Finished suits you if:

  • You want rental income from the first month.
  • You prefer to see exactly what you buy.
  • You have the full amount available to pay quickly.
  • It is your first investment abroad and you want to remove construction risk.

A middle option is to buy into an advanced build: lower delivery risk, a short wait, and a price usually still below the finished product.

12 questions to ask before you reserve

  1. Which company is selling, and where is it registered?
  2. Who owns the land and what certificate does it have (SHM or SHGB)?
  3. What use does the plot's zoning allow? Can it be rented to tourists?
  4. What is the status of land-use validation and the building permit?
  5. Which notary and PPAT is involved?
  6. How many years of leasehold am I buying, and from what date?
  7. Is the extension agreed in the contract? On what terms?
  8. What is the full payment schedule, with amounts and dates?
  9. What are the handover date, grace period and delay compensation?
  10. What does the price include, and what is optional?
  11. Which other projects has the developer delivered? Can I visit them or speak to buyers?
  12. How will I receive construction updates?

If a developer cannot answer any of these clearly, slow down.

Common off-plan mistakes in Bali

  • Deciding on the render. Renders sell; the contract protects you. Read the timing, payment and refund clauses before looking at finishes.
  • Looking only at price. A cheaper leasehold may have fewer years or no agreed extension. Compare the price per year of lease, not just the total.
  • Not checking zoning. A villa on land where tourist rental is not permitted will struggle to operate legally.
  • Calculating yield on high season. Use a prudent average annual occupancy and deduct all costs.
  • Paying through channels the contract does not provide for. Every payment should go to the account in the contract and be documented.
  • Forgetting furniture. If the unit is handed over unfurnished, that cost and lead time come before you can rent.

How long does construction take in Bali?

It depends on project size and the stage at which you buy. As a guide:

  • A one-bedroom villa or studio is usually built in about a year from the start of works.
  • Multi-bedroom villas or complexes with shared areas may need longer.
  • The rainy season (roughly November to March) slows external works.

That is why the contract must include a handover date and a grace period. Buying at an advanced stage shortens the wait, in exchange for a slightly higher price.

Off-plan and returns

An off-plan unit earns nothing until handover. In exchange, the lower entry price improves the projected yield once it is rented. When you run the numbers, factor in:

  • The months of construction without income.
  • The ramp-up time for the rental (photos, listings, first reviews).
  • Management, platform, cleaning, maintenance and tax costs.

Any yield you see on an off-plan project is a projection, not a guarantee. For the wider picture, read our guide to investing in Bali.

Can I sell before handover?

Some off-plan buyers sell their position before completion. Whether that is possible, and on what terms, depends on what the contract says about assigning the right to third parties. If it matters to you, ask before signing and check the contract covers it. Do not buy off-plan counting on flipping before handover: treat it as an option, not the plan.

Glossary

  • Leasehold (Hak Sewa): long-term lease of the land and building, signed before a notary.
  • Freehold (Hak Milik): full land ownership, reserved for Indonesian citizens.
  • SHM / SHGB: land certificates (full ownership or right to build).
  • PPAT: the official who handles deeds on land rights.
  • KKPR: land-use validation for the plot.
  • PBG: building permit.
  • SLF: certificate that the building is fit for use.
  • Pondok Wisata: small-scale tourist accommodation licence.
  • Apostille: legalisation that lets a power of attorney signed abroad be used in Indonesia.

Frequently asked questions

How do you pay for an off-plan villa in Bali?

With a reservation to hold the unit, an initial payment on signing before the notary and the balance in instalments on dates fixed in the contract that follow construction. The final payment is due at handover, and keys are released once the unit is fully paid.

Can I finance an off-plan villa in Bali?

There is no mortgage or loan for foreign leasehold buyers. What spreads the cost is the staged payment schedule during construction, from your own funds and with no interest.

Is buying off-plan in Bali risky?

It carries risks: delays, a developer that does not finish, or land problems. You reduce them by buying from a registered developer with delivered projects, having the notary check the land, insisting on a contract with a handover date, grace period and compensation, and tracking the build through dated reports.

How long does it take to build a villa in Bali?

A one-bedroom villa or studio usually takes about a year from the start of works; larger villas may take longer. The contract should set a handover date and a grace period.

How can I follow construction from abroad?

Our buyers get a client area with their documents, payment schedule and receipts, and receive construction reports with dated photos. They can also visit the site when in Bali or speak to the team by video call.

Is the villa handed over furnished?

It is handed over with all fixtures: kitchen, bathrooms, built-in wardrobes, lighting, pool and air conditioning. Furniture and rental equipment are an optional package quoted separately.

Can the price change during construction?

The price is fixed at signing, together with the payment schedule, and does not change with construction progress. Each contract states its currency; in most of ours it is the euro.
